## @hero-lead
I'm an observational astrophysicist and an Assistant Professor of Practice at the University of the Pacific. Here's how I got here, and what I do when I'm not looking at galaxies.

## @short-bio
Ali Ahmad Khostovan is an Assistant Professor of Practice in the Department of Physics & Astronomy at the University of the Pacific. An observational astrophysicist, he studies how galaxies build up their stars and enrich their gas with heavy elements across cosmic time, using JWST, Hubble, and large ground-based surveys. He has authored <span data-stat="first">10</span> papers, co-authored <span data-stat="coauthored">51</span> more, and leads the COSMOS Spectroscopic Redshift Compilation, a public catalog of nearly half a million galaxy redshifts, as well as the archival redshift compilation for the Roman Space Telescope's GRACE survey. Ali Ahmad earned his BS in Physics in 2012 from UC Irvine under the supervision of Asantha Cooray, and his PhD in Physics in 2018 from UC Riverside under the joint supervision of Bahram Mobasher and David Sobral (University of Lisbon, later Lancaster University), and was a NASA Earth and Space Science Fellowship (NESSF) PhD Fellow from 2016 to 2018. After his PhD, he became a NASA Postdoctoral Program Fellow at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center under the supervision of Sangeeta Malhotra and James Rhoads. In 2021, he became a postdoctoral research associate at the Rochester Institute of Technology working with Jeyhan Kartaltepe, and in 2024 a staff scientist at the University of Kentucky working with Ryan Sanders.

## @story
As a kid, I actually wanted to start my own airline. From our living room in South San Francisco I could watch international flights take off from SFO, and I was hooked on aviation. But I was just as fascinated by space. My dad loved documentaries about the cosmos and I watched them with him, alongside a steady diet of *Star Trek*.

The turning point came in eighth grade, when my science teacher asked what I wanted to be when I grew up. I told her I wanted to run an airline. She replied, “No, I think you are going to be a physicist.” She wasn't far off. I started entering science fairs, earning an honorable mention at the San Mateo County Science Fair, took every physics class I could, and even took astronomy courses at Cañada College while still in high school.

At UC Irvine I joined Asantha Cooray's research group in my first week and spent four years studying dusty, star-forming galaxies with the newly launched Herschel Space Observatory. That led to a PhD at UC Riverside with Bahram Mobasher and David Sobral, which came with regular research trips to Portugal and England, and to everything since.

## @outside
If I weren't an astronomer, I might have become a pilot. I first took the controls of a Cessna at sixteen, as a birthday gift, and have flown from the pilot's seat a few more times since. A private pilot's license is still on my list. I also love plane spotting with my air traffic control radio. There is something deeply relaxing about the orderly choreography of takeoffs and landings, with people heading to and from every corner of the world.

I've played the santur, the Persian hammered dulcimer, for about twenty years (see my [music page](/music/)). I also love long hikes, reading history and poetry, and time with good friends and family.
