## @hero-lead
Growing up in South San Francisco, I met science through the Exploratorium, visits to NASA Ames, and scientists who came to my school. Those experiences made a scientific career feel possible long before I knew how to pursue one. Outreach is how I try to pass that on.

## @why
I see public outreach as part of every astronomer's job. What good is what we learn about the Universe if we keep it to ourselves? Beyond explaining our discoveries, outreach is a chance to spark curiosity, and perhaps to inspire someone to pursue a life in science, whether in academia or industry.

Over the years I've spoken with preschoolers and high-school students, run telescopes for my community, judged student research, and helped build a citizen-science project that lets anyone contribute to real research.

## @featured-wrangler
A Zooniverse project in which thousands of volunteers classify real galaxy spectra. I helped build it, answered volunteers' questions on the forum, and joined office hours to explain how the data were taken and processed.

## @featured-kid-museum
Led a robotics and drone-programming exhibit for elementary- and middle-school children, in partnership with the University of Maryland's Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering.

## @featured-preschool
A talk for preschoolers at the Early Childhood Learning Center in Irvine. Astronomers read, work in teams, help each other, ask lots of questions, and observe the Universe.

## @event-pacific-stem-exploration-day
