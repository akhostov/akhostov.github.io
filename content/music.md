## @hero-lead
Alongside astrophysics, I perform classical Persian music on the santur, the Persian hammered dulcimer. I have played for about twenty years, trained in the instrumental *radif* of Abolhassan Saba and Faramarz Payvar.

## @how-it-started
My first instruments were the piano and later the clarinet, playing Western classical music and jazz. I was introduced to the santur at 13, when my father brought one home from Iran.

My interest in Iranian classical music took hold at 16, after hearing the albums *Night, Silence, Desert* by [Kayhan Kalhor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayhan_Kalhor) and [Mohammad Reza Shajarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad-Reza_Shajarian), and *Mystified* by [Shahram Nazeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahram_Nazeri). At 18, I began formal santur training, studying the instrumental *radif* of maestros [Abolhassan Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolhasan_Saba) and [Faramarz Payvar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faramarz_Payvar), whose style still shapes how I approach the instrument.

## @instrument-intro
A trapezoid-shaped hammered dulcimer with deep historical and cultural roots in Iran. It is an ancestor of the modern piano and is related to instruments around the world, such as the yangqin (China), the cimbalom (Hungary), the salterio (Spain, Portugal, Italy, and Brazil), and the hammered dulcimer (UK and US). It is one of the ancient instruments of Iran and central to classical Iranian music.

## @fact-strings
strings, arranged in 18 groups of four and stretched across a wooden soundbox over movable bridges.

## @fact-hammers
featherweight wooden hammers, called *mezrab*, held between the fingers and struck against the strings.

## @fact-years
years of playing and training in the classical Persian repertoire.

## @tradition
Classical Persian music is built on the [*radif*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radif_(music)), a body of traditional melodies organized into modal systems called [*dastgāh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dastgah). Performances move between composed pieces and improvisation within these modes, so no two are quite the same.

## @physics
Each santur string rings at a precise set of harmonic frequencies, and the tuning can be changed either by moving the bridges (*kharak*) or by adjusting the tension of the string. I have used my santur and other instruments for demonstrations in my physics courses, and in a Physics of Music course at UC Irvine, where I was a guest lecturer (2013).
