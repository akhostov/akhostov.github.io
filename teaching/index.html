Teaching

Every student can learn physics

I believe every student can learn physics and astronomy when given clear explanations, structure, and encouragement. My goal is for students to leave able to reason through problems on their own, in physics and well beyond it.

How I teach

Active, concrete, and explicit

I introduce the underlying idea, connect it to something familiar, and then work through problems by naming what we know, what we need, and why each principle applies, before any calculation.

Active learning

Students solve problems in class and discuss their approaches with each other. I model the reasoning first, then let them practice it, consistent with strong evidence that active learning improves outcomes for every student.

Physics you can see

Swiffer dusters explain electrostatics, cars turning on wet and dry roads connect circular motion with friction, and blowing across bottle openings demonstrates resonance. Making connections between concepts in class and everyday experiences allows students to truly understand the physics.

Clear expectations for everyone

I make expectations and problem-solving strategies explicit, treat struggle as a normal part of learning, and offer several ways to engage with the material, so that every student, whatever their background, knows what success looks like and how to get there.

AI as a study partner

I want students to use generative AI as a tool in the learning process, not as a replacement for it. I encourage them to use it to ask follow-up questions, clarify difficult concepts, and generate practice problems, while the reasoning and problem solving remain their own.
