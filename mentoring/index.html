Approach

How I mentor

Every student arrives with different experience and goals, so I adapt my mentoring to each one, from a first research project to a first-author paper.

I tailor my guidance to each student, combining regular one-on-one meetings with an open-door policy, in person, over Slack, and on Zoom. When a student hits a hard problem, I help break it into manageable questions and give them time to develop and explain their own approach. Those conversations sometimes run past an hour, and they are where students build the confidence to tackle the next problem alone. I learn from their methods and perspectives too: mentoring is a partnership.

For new researchers I offer well-defined projects with achievable milestones, then support the move to open-ended work. I am explicit about authorship and collaboration expectations, and I help with scientific writing, presentations, fellowship applications, and career planning.

Learning forums

Where rank doesn't matter

astroTopics

A weekly forum I founded during my PhD at UC Riverside (2017–2018) and restarted at RIT (2022–2024). Participants vote on a topic, spend a week researching it on their own through papers, books, and lectures, then meet to share what they learned. I moderate so that academic rank stays out of the way: students ask questions and contribute alongside postdocs and faculty.

RIT Galaxy Evolution Journal Club

I founded and organized a journal club (2022–2024) in which students led the discussion of new papers. Students reported more confidence discussing complex ideas and approaching senior colleagues.
