First-author papers

Papers I led, each with a one-line summary in plain language.

  1. The AURORA Survey: The Mass–Metallicity and Fundamental Metallicity Relations at z ∼ 2.3 Based Purely on Direct Te Metallicities

    A. A. Khostovan, R. L. Sanders, et al.

    ApJL, 1003, L41 (2026)

    In briefDirect-method (electron-temperature) oxygen abundances for 34 galaxies at z ≈ 1.4–3.5 from JWST/AURORA give a calibration-free mass–metallicity relation at cosmic noon. None of six cosmological simulations reproduces its evolution since z ≈ 2.

    ADSarXivDOI

  2. COSMOS Spectroscopic Redshift Compilation (First Data Release): 488k Redshifts Encompassing Two Decades of Spectroscopy

    A. A. Khostovan, J. Kartaltepe, et al.

    ApJS, 282, 6 (2026)

    In briefA public compilation of 487,666 spectroscopic redshifts for 266,284 objects in COSMOS from 138 programs, with self-organizing maps identifying the galaxy populations that still lack spectroscopic coverage.

    ADSarXivDOIData

  3. EELG1002: A Record-Breaking [O III]+Hβ EW ∼ 3700 Å Galaxy at z ∼ 0.8 – Analog of Early Galaxies?

    A. A. Khostovan, J. Kartaltepe, et al.

    ApJ, 994, 34 (2025)

    In briefEELG1002, identified in archival Gemini spectra, has a record [O III]+Hβ equivalent width of 3,100–3,700 Å, 32–36 times typical for its mass and epoch, and properties closely matching reionization-era galaxies.

    ADSarXiv

  4. Evolution of Hα Equivalent Widths from z ∼ 0.4–2.2: implications for star formation and legacy surveys with Roman and Euclid

    A. A. Khostovan, S. Malhotra, J. Rhoads, et al.

    MNRAS, 535, 2903 (2024)

    In briefForward modeling of Hα equivalent widths at z ≈ 0.4–2.2 shows that galaxies with bursty star formation produced 40–60% of all new stars at cosmic noon, with forecasts for Roman and Euclid grism surveys.

    ADSarXiv

  5. Correlations between Hα Equivalent Width and Galaxy Properties at z = 0.47: Physical or Selection-Driven?

    A. A. Khostovan, S. Malhotra, J. Rhoads, et al.

    MNRAS, 503, 5115 (2021)

    In briefForward modeling of survey selection shows that the Hα equivalent width–stellar mass anticorrelation is intrinsic but shallower than observed (∝ M−0.16), and that low-mass galaxies are intrinsically burstier.

    ADSarXivDOI

  6. A large, deep 3 deg² survey of Hα, [O III], and [O II] emitters from LAGER: constraining luminosity functions

    A. A. Khostovan, S. Malhotra, J. Rhoads, et al.

    MNRAS, 493, 3966 (2020)

    In briefHα, [O III], and [O II] luminosity functions and star-formation rate densities at z = 0.47, 0.93, and 1.59 from LAGER, one of the deepest narrowband surveys to date.

    ADSarXivDOI

  7. The clustering of typical Lyα emitters from z ∼ 2.5–6: host halo masses depend on Lyα and UV luminosities

    A. A. Khostovan, D. Sobral, B. Mobasher, et al.

    MNRAS, 489, 555 (2019)

    In briefThe clustering of about 5,000 Lyα emitters at z ≈ 2.5–6 reveals a redshift-independent relation between line luminosity (relative to L*) and dark matter halo mass.

    ADSarXivDOI

  8. The clustering of Hβ+[O III] and [O II] emitters since z ∼ 5: dependencies with line luminosity and stellar mass

    A. A. Khostovan, D. Sobral, B. Mobasher, et al.

    MNRAS, 478, 2999 (2018)

    In briefThe clustering of about 7,000 Hβ+[O III] and [O II] emitters to z ≈ 5 reveals a redshift-independent relation between line luminosity (relative to L*) and dark matter halo mass.

    ADSarXivDOI

  9. The nature of Hβ+[O III] and [O II] emitters to z ∼ 5 with HiZELS: stellar mass functions and the evolution of EWs

    A. A. Khostovan, D. Sobral, B. Mobasher, et al.

    MNRAS, 463, 2363 (2016)

    In briefStellar mass functions and equivalent-width evolution for about 7,000 Hβ+[O III] and [O II] emitters to z ≈ 5, implying higher ionization parameters in high-redshift galaxies.

    ADSarXivDOIPress release

  10. Evolution of the Hβ+[O III] and [O II] Luminosity Functions and the [O II] Star-Formation History of the Universe up to z ∼ 5
